What do these terms mean?

Total hypothetical capital available to the account. It does not mean the entire amount is invested in one position.

Maximum planned loss if the hypothetical stop-loss is reached. For example, 1% of $10,000 creates a $100 risk budget.

The assumed price of one share or unit when the simulated position begins.

The planned exit level when price moves against the hypothetical position. Actual market execution can differ because of gaps, liquidity and slippage.

Average True Range measures recent historical price movement. MarketPulse can use it to create a volatility-adjusted stop-loss distance.

An optional favourable exit price used to compare potential reward with the calculated risk.
Calculation Method
How does MarketPulse calculate the risk plan?
1 Maximum Planned Loss

Trading Capital × Maximum Risk Percentage

$10,000 × 1% = $100
2 Risk per Unit

Absolute difference between Entry Price and Stop Price.

$315.42 − $299.65 = $15.77
3 Position Size

Maximum Planned Loss ÷ Risk per Unit

$100 ÷ $15.77 ≈ 6 units
The calculation is a risk-planning simulation. It does not guarantee the actual loss because real execution may be affected by market gaps, liquidity and slippage.

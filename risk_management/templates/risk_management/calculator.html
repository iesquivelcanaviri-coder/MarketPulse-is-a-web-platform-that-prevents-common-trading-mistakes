What do these inputs mean?

Use these explanations if you are unfamiliar with trade-risk terminology.

Total hypothetical capital available to the trading account. It does not mean this entire amount will be invested in one position.

This is the maximum planned loss if the simulated stop-loss is reached. Example: Capital = $10,000 Risk = 1% Maximum planned loss = $100. It does not mean only $100 is invested.

The assumed price of one share or unit when the hypothetical trade begins. MarketPulse can populate this using Alpaca's latest available trade price.

The stop-loss is the planned exit level when price moves against the hypothetical trade. Actual market execution can differ because of price gaps, liquidity and slippage.

Average True Range measures recent historical price movement. MarketPulse can use ATR to calculate a stop distance that responds to the asset's recent volatility.

An optional favourable exit price. It allows MarketPulse to compare possible reward with the amount placed at risk.

Why use a stress test if I already have a stop-loss?
The normal risk planner assumes a predefined stop level. Stress testing asks a different question: what happens if markets move unusually quickly, volatility increases sharply, liquidity falls, or historical market behaviour changes? A real stop order is not guaranteed to execute exactly at the requested price during severe market conditions.

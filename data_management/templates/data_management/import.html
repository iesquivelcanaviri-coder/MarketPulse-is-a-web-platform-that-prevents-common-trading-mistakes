How does Market Condition work?
1
Check Historical Coverage

MarketPulse checks how many historical {{ selected_symbol }} observations are available in core.MarketData. At least 60 observations are required by the current classification model.

2
Refresh From Alpaca If Required

When historical coverage is insufficient, the backend can request additional recent daily OHLCV bars from Alpaca and store or update them in PostgreSQL.

3
Calculate Returns and Volatility

MarketPulse calculates historical daily percentage returns and annualises their variability to estimate historical volatility.

4
Compare Moving Averages

The analytical engine calculates a 20-period moving average and a 60-period moving average and uses their relationship with the latest stored closing price to evaluate recent trend structure.

5
Classify the Market Condition

The internal Market Regime Analysis classifies the historical environment as Uptrend, Downtrend, Sideways or High Volatility and saves the result in PostgreSQL.

Backend flow:
Alpaca → data_management/services/alpaca.py → core.MarketData → data_management.views.market_condition() → identify_market_regime() → analysis_tools.models.MarketRegime → PostgreSQL → Detailed Market Condition panel.

Data source and calculation

Provider: {{ market_data_provider|default:"Alpaca" }} · Feed: {{ market_data_feed|default:"IEX" }}

Alpaca supplies historical market bars. MarketPulse normalises those observations and persists Open, High, Low, Close and Volume records in core.MarketData .

Market Condition does not classify an asset from one live quote. It requires a sequence of historical observations because trend and volatility describe behaviour across time.

The current analytical engine requires at least 60 observations because it calculates both a 20-period and 60-period moving average.

Daily return is calculated by comparing each closing price with the previous closing price.

Historical volatility is calculated from the variation of those daily returns and annualised using the approximate number of trading sessions in one year.

The final classification is created by: identify_market_regime() and persisted as a MarketRegime record so the result can be reused by other MarketPulse features.

Historical analysis only. This classification describes behaviour detected in historical observations for {{ selected_symbol }}. It does not predict future prices, recommend a trade or constitute personalised financial, investment or trading advice.
